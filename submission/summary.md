# Fire Continuum — Read the fire season

Fire Continuum connects NASA’s MODIS and VIIRS records into a burning activity calendar that helps users ask three questions: When does burning usually peak? Is this period unusual? What evidence supports the answer?

The geographic workbench spans 185 eligible country/territory entries from 2003–2025, with a separate November 1–13, 2026 comparison against exactly the same dates historically. 5,550 annual NASA files underpin the country histories, and twenty-six daily files underpin the worldwide recent snapshot. An interactive map, effective area selection, calendar, seasonal briefing, exact-date analytics, original NASA annual-source links and a reproducible evidence ZIP turn the record into an inspectable workflow.

A curated southern Borneo case supplies a clear independent success. On 15 September 2019, Landsat reveals a bright infrared fire front and smoke plume; sixteen NOAA-20 centres from 3 hours 47 minutes later follow the same area. Users reveal infrared and toggle the later observations themselves. This corroborates burning at that place and date, not precise counts or model accuracy. The earlier six-cell audit remains separately published with all six outcomes inconclusive.

The larger Kalimantan September episode is above typical in both instruments and all six processing variants. Calibration is tested on years excluded from fitting, including decision-category agreement and rare-category failures. Punjab and southeast Australia transfer experiments show where local adaptation helps and where it does not.

Every country is screened independently: 9 agreement checks passed, 7 limited rare-event evidence, 109 calibration failed checks, 60 insufficient evidence. Failed or unsupported calibration uses an Aqua-only history while retaining the candidate diagnostics. A reusable data-package contract supports additional geographies. A local AI evidence assistant explains selected measurements with source citations, using semantic retrieval rather than freely generated scientific claims. The simple homepage gives a plain-language answer first, with sources and technical details on demand. Users can save six places, compare their own seasonal baselines and download a briefing. The assistant also runs explicit analysis tasks through deterministic functions. Existing detailed research views remain accessible. A plain-language glossary and complete handbook explain every unit, assumption and limit. Sources, model versions and file hashes travel with the exported analysis.

New October 2026 observations extend Mexico and Australia using 62 verified daily files and unchanged models. Both retain category agreement, but count differences persist. A fixed Mexico full-mask/QA audit distinguishes cloud, clear land and sun glint; its 125-minute pass separation fails the declared timing rule, so coverage normalization is withheld. A voluntary local feedback form prepares usability testing without claiming completed practitioner validation.

The result is a reproducible research prototype, not a warning system or globally validated fire census. Continuous 2026 coverage, paired clear-sky normalization and practitioner validation remain future work. NASA data drive every finding; NASA endorsement is not claimed.
